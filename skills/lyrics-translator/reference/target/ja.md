# Japanese Target Lyrics

## Output Rules

- Write idiomatic Japanese with natural SOV order, particles, and sentence endings.
- Select pronouns, honorific distance, and final particles only when voice and relationship are established by the lyric.
- Match the song's register: casual lyrics take colloquial endings (だ / ちゃう / たい), not stiff written forms (書き言葉・硬い漢語).
- Render foreign names and loanwords consistently; use Japanese wording for meaning rather than mechanically copying source syntax.
- Preserve repeated hooks and parallelism with comparable mora rhythm.

## Native Voice Checklist

Apply during the critique pass (common.md Step 5):

- Match the song's spoken register. For pop/rock lyrics, avoid stiff Sino-Japanese compounds (漢語表現) and written-style endings (である / ございます); prefer spoken phrasing (だよ / じゃない / してる).
- Avoid particle stacking: chains like のためにのことを sound bureaucratic. Restructure or omit particles where natural speech would.
- Run a connotation check on emotionally loaded words: 孤独 (literary isolation) vs ひとりぼっち (felt loneliness) — pick the register the source carries.
- Keep translationese out: no mirrored source-language word order (SVO leaking into SOV), no unnatural は/が confusion from translating "is/are" mechanically, no explanatory のだ where bare statement suffices.
- Pronouns: Japanese lyrics frequently omit 私/君. Do not add pronouns just because the source language states them — omit when the subject is clear from context or mood.

## Rhythm and Length

- Prefer concise lines of 20 mora or fewer. Expand up to 35 mora only when the source line and melody clearly support it; never exceed 40.
- Match strong beats and breath groups, not literal syllable counts.
- Do not fill a short source line with explanatory particles or modifiers.
