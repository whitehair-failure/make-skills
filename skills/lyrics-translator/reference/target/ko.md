# Korean Target Lyrics

## Output Rules

- Write idiomatic Korean with appropriate particles, connective endings, and speech level.
- Carry source honorifics and intimacy as Korean register, not literal grammatical labels.
- Match speech level to the song's voice: casual lyrics take 반말 endings, not literary or formal ones.
- Render culture-specific terms and sound words by their contextual effect; avoid gratuitous transliteration.
- Keep repeated hooks and parallel phrases balanced in syllable count and register.

## Native Voice Checklist

Apply during the critique pass (common.md Step 5):

- Speech level lock: once the song establishes 반말 or 존댓말 in the first section, maintain it throughout. Mixing 해체 with 하십시오체 mid-song breaks immersion unless the lyric deliberately shifts voice.
- Avoid direct-translation particles: mechanical 은/는 and 을/를 insertion from source SVO order sounds stiff. Korean lyrics frequently drop particles for rhythm — follow that convention when natural.
- Connotation check on Sino-Korean (한자어) vs native Korean (고유어): 고독(孤獨) is literary/heavy; 외로움 is felt/personal. Pick the register the source carries.
- Keep translationese out: no unnatural subject-first order copied from English/Chinese, no 것이다 chains for simple statements, no 의 stacking (Korean equivalent of 的-chains).
- Emotion words: Korean lyrics lean on specific feel-words (설레다, 아련하다, 먹먹하다) that have no clean 1:1 equivalents. Choose the Korean word by emotional texture, not dictionary definition.

## Rhythm and Length

- Aim for 4–16 Hangul syllables per line; never exceed 18.
- Match strong beats and breath groups rather than literal source syllable counts.
- Keep short hooks short and avoid padding with extra endings.
