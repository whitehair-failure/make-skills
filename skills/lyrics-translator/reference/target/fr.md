# French Target Lyrics

## Output Rules

- Write flowing, musical French with natural prosody and euphonic flow.
- Maintain consistent address: lock in _tu_ or _vous_ based on the song's intimate or theatrical stance; never toggle randomly mid-song.
- Favor active, evocative verbs over nominalized, bureaucratized phrasing.
- Respect poetic elisions (_j'_, _l'_, _d'_, _qu'_) to maintain smooth vocal legato.

## Native Voice Checklist

Apply during the critique pass (common.md Step 5):

- **No Translationese**: Avoid English-style passive voice (_il a été frappé par la nouvelle_ → prefer _la nouvelle l'a bouleversé_).
- **Subordinate Clause Density**: Avoid piling up _que / qui_ clauses that make lyric lines stiff and breathless.
- **Register Match**: For modern French pop/urban music, use contemporary natural wording; avoid archaisms (_point_, _guère_, _passé simple_) unless translating historic musicals or classical chanson.
- **Rhythmic Musicality**: French accents naturally fall on the final syllable of rhythmic breath groups; align key emotional words with phrase endings.

## Rhythm and Length

- Aim for 6–15 pronounced syllables per line; rarely exceed 17.
- Account for whether final _-e_ will be sung or elided depending on the musical tempo.
- Keep rhyming or assonance supple (rimes croisées, suivies or embrassées) when in cover/singing mode.
