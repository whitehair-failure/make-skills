# French Target Lyrics

## Output Rules

- Write flowing, musical French with natural prosody and euphonic flow.
- Maintain consistent address: lock in *tu* or *vous* based on the song's intimate or theatrical stance; never toggle randomly mid-song.
- Favor active, evocative verbs over nominalized, bureaucratized phrasing.
- Respect poetic elisions (*j'*, *l'*, *d'*, *qu'*) to maintain smooth vocal legato.

## Native Voice Checklist

Apply during the critique pass (common.md Step 5):

- **No Translationese**: Avoid English-style passive voice (*il a été frappé par la nouvelle* → prefer *la nouvelle l'a bouleversé*).
- **Subordinate Clause Density**: Avoid piling up *que / qui* clauses that make lyric lines stiff and breathless.
- **Register Match**: For modern French pop/urban music, use contemporary natural wording; avoid archaisms (*point*, *guère*, *passé simple*) unless translating historic musicals or classical chanson.
- **Rhythmic Musicality**: French accents naturally fall on the final syllable of rhythmic breath groups; align key emotional words with phrase endings.

## Rhythm and Length

- Aim for 6–15 pronounced syllables per line; rarely exceed 17.
- Account for whether final *-e* will be sung or elided depending on the musical tempo.
- Keep rhyming or assonance supple (rimes croisées, suivies or embrassées) when in cover/singing mode.
