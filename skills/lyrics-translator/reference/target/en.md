# English Target Lyrics

## Output Rules

- Prefer direct, idiomatic English with a clear verb and natural stress pattern.
- Restore a subject only when English grammar or the established narrative requires it; do not over-explain source-language ellipsis.
- Use contractions and informal phrasing only when they match the song’s register.
- Match the song's register throughout: conversational lyrics stay conversational (contractions, plain verbs); do not elevate them into formal or literary English.
- Render culture-specific terms, onomatopoeia, and particles by effect or meaning, not romanization.

## Native Voice Checklist

Apply during the critique pass (common.md Step 5):

- Watch for translationese: inverted or awkward word order copied from the source language, over-nominalization ("the doing of X" instead of "doing X"), and unnecessary passives ("it was felt by me" instead of "I felt").
- Register consistency: if the first verse uses contractions and plain verbs, don't suddenly switch to formal or literary phrasing mid-song. The register set in verse 1 carries through unless a clear dramatic shift justifies the change.
- Avoid over-explanation: source languages often leave things implicit. English can too — "lost in you" is stronger than "I have become lost because of you." Trust the listener.
- Connotation check before locking emotionally loaded words: "lonely" (neutral ache) vs "desolate" (dramatic, literary) vs "alone" (factual) — pick the weight the source carries.
- No filler words for syllable padding: avoid inserting "just", "so", "really", "oh" purely to fill rhythmic gaps unless they match the song's voice.

## Rhythm and Length

- Aim for 6–16 syllables per line; never exceed 18.
- Match strong beats and parallel structure rather than exact source syllable counts.
- Keep short hooks short. Preserve repeated lines with identical wording unless context changes their meaning.
