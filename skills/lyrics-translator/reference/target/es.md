# Spanish Target Lyrics

## Output Rules

- Write vibrant, natural Spanish with fluid rhythmic cadences and natural metric accentuation.
- Leverage the pro-drop nature of Spanish: omit unnecessary subject pronouns (_yo_, _tú_) unless rhythmic meter or dramatic contrast requires them.
- Use natural rhythmic stress: Spanish lyrics naturally emphasize penultimate-syllable stress (_palabras llanas_ or _graves_), which comfortably matches feminine musical downbeats.
- Match register: decide whether the song calls for informal Latin American Spanish (_tú/ustedes_) or Iberian European Spanish (_tú/vosotros_) and maintain consistency.

## Native Voice Checklist

Apply during the critique pass (common.md Step 5):

- **No Translationese**: Avoid calquing English/Germanic passives (_fue amado por mí_ → prefer _yo lo amaba_ or _lo quise tanto_).
- **Sinalefa Optimization**: When aiming for singability, utilize natural vowel merging (_sinalefa_) to fit syllables smoothly into tight rhythmic bars.
- **Natural Word Order**: Favor emotional topic placement over rigid SVO structures.
- **Idiomatic Slang & Passion**: Avoid bookish or stiff academic phrasing; Spanish song lyrics thrive on raw emotional immediacy (_el alma_, _el corazón_, _la piel_).

## Rhythm and Length

- Aim for 6–16 pronounced syllables per line; rarely exceed 18.
- Account for _sinalefa_ when calculating vocal singability.
- In cover/singing mode, aim for assonant (_asonante_) or consonant (_consonante_) rhymes at phrase endings.
