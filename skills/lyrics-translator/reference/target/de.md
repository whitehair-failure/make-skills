# German Target Lyrics

## Output Rules

- Write dynamic, idiomatic German with strong rhythmic pulse and natural metric stress (Trochaic, Iambic, or Dactylic).
- Keep word order musically viable: avoid overly complex subordinate clauses that push crucial verbs into unaccented, breathless line corners.
- Express verbal actions cleanly; avoid bureaucratic nominal style (*Nominalstil* like *das Empfinden von Schmerz* → write *den Schmerz spüren*).
- Maintain consistent address: lock in *du* for personal songs unless an aristocratic or detached narrative demands *Sie*.

## Native Voice Checklist

Apply during the critique pass (common.md Step 5):

- **No Translationese**: Avoid calquing English continuous aspect (*ich bin am Machen* in inappropriate poetic registers) or artificial passive voices.
- **Compound Word Restraint**: Do not build unpronounceable mega-compounds that choke the vocal line; break dense ideas into melodic phrases.
- **Stress-Beat Synchronization**: Ensure strong German syllables (stressed root vowels) fall on musical downbeats, while grammatical endings (*-en*, *-er*, *-e*) fall on weak upbeats.
- **Natural Colloquial Cadence**: For German pop/indie songs, embrace natural contractions (*gibt's*, *seh'*, *hab'*, *auf'm*) to fit natural breath points.

## Rhythm and Length

- Aim for 5–15 syllables per line; rarely exceed 16.
- German syllables carry heavy consonant clusters; leave breathing room between rapid percussive phrases.
- In cover/singing mode, prioritize masculine (single stressed beat) or feminine (stressed + unstressed) line endings that match the musical cadence.
