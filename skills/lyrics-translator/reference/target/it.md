# Italian Target Lyrics

## Output Rules

- Write fluid, sonorous Italian with open vowels and natural melodic resonance (*bel canto* suitability).
- Respect natural penult accentuation (*parole piane* - stress on second-to-last syllable) which dominates Italian poetic rhythm.
- Use truncation (*troncamento*: *cuor*, *amor*) judiciously when the musical meter requires a crisp masculine (single-beat) ending.
- Maintain consistent address: use *tu* for intimate/romantic songs, reserving *Lei* or *voi* for formal period pieces.

## Native Voice Checklist

Apply during the critique pass (common.md Step 5):

- **No Translationese**: Avoid calquing English subject-first rigidity; let the verb and natural vocal cadence lead.
- **Double Consonant & Vowel Balance**: Ensure Italian double consonants (*tt*, *ll*, *rr*) fall on rhythmic beats where singers can comfortably articulate them.
- **Clitic Positioning**: Check that combined pronouns (*glielo*, *mene*) sound natural and do not clutter the musical line.
- **Evocative Emotional Lexicon**: Choose words rich in Italian musical tradition (*passione*, *sguardo*, *destino*, *nostalgia*).

## Rhythm and Length

- Aim for 6–15 syllables per line; rarely exceed 16.
- Leverage vowel elision across word boundaries (*sinalefe*) to maintain vocal fluidity.
- In cover/singing mode, aim for musical end-rhymes (baciate, alternate, incrociate).
