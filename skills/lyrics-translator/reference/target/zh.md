# Chinese Target Lyrics

## Output Rules

- Use concise, natural Chinese; omit pronouns and function words when they add no meaning.
- Convey tense, aspect, modality, and register only when semantically needed.
- Translate idioms, cultural terms, and sound words for meaning and musical effect. Avoid untranslated Japanese or Korean romanization.
- Preserve parallel hooks with comparable character counts where possible.

## Native Voice Checklist

Apply during the critique pass (common.md Step 5):

- Match the song's spoken register. For conversational youth lyrics, avoid bookish connectives and formal four-character expressions (无济于事 / 并非 / 若要); prefer spoken phrasing (无论怎么努力都做不到).
- Run a connotation check before locking any emotionally loaded word (浮躁 = negative restlessness vs. 雀跃 / 心里痒痒的 = sweet giddiness — pick the color the source carries).
- Keep translationese out: no 的-chains, no Europeanized passives, no added logical connectives (因此 / 所以) the source does not state.
- No invented parallel halves (尝不出酸甜 + 摸不清好坏 — "好坏" invented) and no invented head nouns (迷路的小孩 — "小孩" invented).

## Rhythm and Length

- Prefer 4–16 Chinese characters per line; 18 is the normal hard ceiling and 20 is an exceptional maximum when meaning would otherwise be lost.
- Match beats and breath groups rather than translating every source syllable.
- Do not pad short lines with decorative modifiers.
