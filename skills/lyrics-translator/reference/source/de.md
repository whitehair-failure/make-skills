# German Source Lyrics

## Read Before Translating

- **Klammerform (Sentence Bracket & Verb Placement)**: German lyrics frequently place separable prefixes or conjugated auxiliary/modal verbs at the start of a clause while burying the infinitive/participle or separable prefix at the very end of the line (or on the next line). Always assemble the full verbal frame before translating!
  - _Example_: _Ich fange deine Tränen... auf_ (_auffangen_ = to catch).
- **Subordinate Clause Verb-Final Order**: In clauses introduced by _weil_, _dass_, _wenn_, _als_, _ob_, the conjugated verb sits at the end, often across enjambed lyric lines.
- **Compound Nouns (Komposita)**: German freely fuses concepts into singular compounds (_Herzzerreißend_, _Sehnsuchtsort_, _Gefühlschaos_). Deconstruct the semantic core and render it naturally rather than creating awkward hyphenated calques.
- **Konjunktiv II (Subjunctive)**: Carries deep emotional weight in Lied and musical lyrics, denoting longing, unfulfilled desire, or wistful regret (_wärst du doch hier_, _ich könnte fliegen_).

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain:

- A separable verb prefix waiting on the following line (_Ich gebe diesen Kampf nicht... / auf_).
- An auxiliary verb (_habe_, _bin_, _wird_) with its past participle or infinitive on the next line.
- A subordinate connective (_obwohl_, _während_, _damit_) spanning multiple rhythmic bars.
- Relative clauses introduced by _der/die/das/dessen/denen_.

## Common Traps

- Never translate line-by-line when a separable verb or auxiliary bracket spans multiple lines.
- Do not let heavy consonant clusters (_str_, _pf_, _cht_) mislead you into thinking the song is harsh; German lyric poetry often conveys profound vulnerability and tenderness (_Zärtlichkeit_).
- Distinguish formal _Sie_ (rare in pop lyrics, present in theatrical/satirical pieces) from familiar _du_ / _ihr_.
