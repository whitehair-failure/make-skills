# Spanish Source Lyrics

## Read Before Translating

- **Sinalefa (Metrical Synalepha)**: In Spanish song lyrics, when a word ends with a vowel and the next word begins with a vowel (or silent _h_), they fuse into a **single spoken/sung syllable**. A line written with 12 graphic words may only have 7 or 8 sung beats.
- **Subject Ellipsis**: Spanish is a pro-drop language; conjugated verbs encode person and number (_tengo_, _sabes_, _quiere_). Explicit pronouns (_yo_, _tú_, _ella_) are inserted only for dramatic emphasis or contrast.
- **Enclitic Pronoun Binding**: Pronouns attach to infinitives, gerunds, and imperatives (_diciéndotelo_, _mírame_, _olvidarme_).
- **Double Negation**: Standard Spanish grammar uses double negatives (_no quiero nada_, _nunca he visto a nadie_); these are intensifications, not positive logic.
- **Regional Variants**: Distinguish Iberian Spanish (_vosotros_, distinction /θ/) from Latin American Spanish (_ustedes_, seseo, regional urban slang like _parce_, _chaval_, _morra_).

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain:

- A prepositional phrase or subordinate conjunction (_aunque_, _para que_, _mientras_, _cuando_) completed on the next line.
- An enjambment splitting a verb from its direct object or gerund phrase (_sigo / pensando en ti_).
- Conditional constructs (_si tú me miras... / yo me vuelvo loco_).
- Anaphora and repeated parallel clauses typical of reggaeton or flamenco coplas.

## Common Traps

- Do not interpret double negation as a mathematical positive.
- Beware of false cognates with English or Portuguese (_éxito_ = hit/success, not exit; _embarazada_ = pregnant, not embarrassed; _actualmente_ = currently, not actually).
- Do not invent explicit pronouns where Spanish relies on the dramatic intimacy of verb endings.
