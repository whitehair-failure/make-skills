# Thai Source Lyrics

## Read Before Translating

- **Tonal Isolating Language**: Thai has five tones (mid, low, falling, high, rising). In contemporary pop songs, melodies often alter or bend spoken lexical tones to fit musical notes; interpret words by contextual vocabulary, not pitch transcription alone.
- **Script Without Word Spacing**: Thai writing does not use spaces between words; spaces function as punctuation or breath breaks. Carefully segment compound words and idioms.
- **Complex Pronoun & Kinship System**: Thai lyrics feature highly nuanced personal pronouns reflecting gender, intimacy, modesty, or status (_ฉัน_, _เธอ_, _พี่_, _น้อง_, _กู_, _มึง_, _เค้า_, _ตัวเอง_). Map them to appropriate target-language registers rather than generic "I/you".
- **Sentence-Final Mood Particles**: Words like _นะ_, _สิ_, _จ้ะ_, _ครับ/ค่ะ_, _เลย_, _หรอก_ carry essential emotional shading (pleading, softening, urging, teasing, denial). Never ignore them as meaningless noise.

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain:

- A continuous syntactic phrase written without terminal spacing across lines.
- Topic-comment structures where the descriptive modifier is placed on the following line.
- Serial verb constructions (_ไปหา_, _คิดถึง_, _รอคอย_) spanning adjacent lines.
- Conjunctions like _แม้ว่า_ (even though), _ถ้าหาก_ (if), _เพราะว่า_ (because) completed in subsequent bars.

## Common Traps

- Do not mistake kinship terms (_พี่_ older sibling / senior, _น้อง_ younger sibling / junior) for literal biological family in romance lyrics.
- Beware of slang and abbreviations common in T-Pop and indie music.
- Do not let the lack of explicit verb tenses mislead you; determine past, present, or future aspect from context particles (_แล้ว_, _กำลัง_, _จะ_).
