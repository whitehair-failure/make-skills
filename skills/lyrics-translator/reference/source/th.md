# Thai Source Lyrics

## Read Before Translating

- **Tonal Isolating Language**: Thai has five tones (mid, low, falling, high, rising). In contemporary pop songs, melodies often alter or bend spoken lexical tones to fit musical notes; interpret words by contextual vocabulary, not pitch transcription alone.
- **Script Without Word Spacing**: Thai writing does not use spaces between words; spaces function as punctuation or breath breaks. Carefully segment compound words and idioms.
- **Complex Pronoun & Kinship System**: Thai lyrics feature highly nuanced personal pronouns reflecting gender, intimacy, modesty, or status (*ฉัน*, *เธอ*, *พี่*, *น้อง*, *กู*, *มึง*, *เค้า*, *ตัวเอง*). Map them to appropriate target-language registers rather than generic "I/you".
- **Sentence-Final Mood Particles**: Words like *นะ*, *สิ*, *จ้ะ*, *ครับ/ค่ะ*, *เลย*, *หรอก* carry essential emotional shading (pleading, softening, urging, teasing, denial). Never ignore them as meaningless noise.

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain:

- A continuous syntactic phrase written without terminal spacing across lines.
- Topic-comment structures where the descriptive modifier is placed on the following line.
- Serial verb constructions (*ไปหา*, *คิดถึง*, *รอคอย*) spanning adjacent lines.
- Conjunctions like *แม้ว่า* (even though), *ถ้าหาก* (if), *เพราะว่า* (because) completed in subsequent bars.

## Common Traps

- Do not mistake kinship terms (*พี่* older sibling / senior, *น้อง* younger sibling / junior) for literal biological family in romance lyrics.
- Beware of slang and abbreviations common in T-Pop and indie music.
- Do not let the lack of explicit verb tenses mislead you; determine past, present, or future aspect from context particles (*แล้ว*, *กำลัง*, *จะ*).
