# Korean Source Lyrics

## Read Before Translating

- Resolve particles, connective endings, tense/aspect, honorifics, and speech level from the whole block.
- Translate culture-bound terms such as `오빠`, `한`, `정`, and `눈치` by their meaning in context, not default transliteration.
- Treat 의성어/의태어 and code-switched lines as semantic and rhythmic choices, not noise.

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain any of these:

- A connective or verb-ending stack such as `-고`, `-며`, `-는데`, `-아서/어서`, or `-듯` that lacks closure.
- A dependent modifier ending in `-는`, `-던`, or `-ㄴ/은` whose head follows.
- An omitted subject/object or honorific relation resolved only by nearby lines.
- A code-switched phrase whose role is determined by the surrounding Korean text.

## Common Traps

- Carry speech level as target-language register, not as a literal suffix.
- Recognize truncated 관용구/속담 and hanja-based idioms; resolve the full idiom's intent before translating (cf. Sino-Korean false friends in pair-exceptions).
- Leave a wholly English source line unchanged; translate mixed lines by their overall meaning.
- Do not transliterate Korean sound words when an effect word is natural in the target.
