# Generic Source Lyrics (Universal Fallback)

Used when the source language does not have a dedicated `reference/source/{source}.md` profile. Applies to any world language (e.g. Arabic, Hindi, Swedish, Dutch, Greek, Turkish, Tagalog, Swahili, etc.).

## Read Before Translating

- Identify language family, script, and basic typology (SVO / SOV / VSO, isolating / agglutinative / fusional).
- Observe lineation and musical phrasing: lyrics in any language structure meaning around breath pauses, verse–chorus alternations, and emotional climaxes.
- Do not translate word-by-word. Unpack idiomatic expressions, cultural references, and slang into their intended emotional and communicative function.
- Identify the voice: gender, social distance, intimacy level, and narrative perspective (I / you / we / they).

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain:

- Run-on syntax or enjambment: a prepositional phrase, relative clause, or direct object carried over into the next line.
- An unfinished clause or dependent connective (e.g., "because...", "when...", "if...").
- A modifier whose modified head noun/verb appears on the following line.
- Repeated parallel hooks or refrains designed to deliver a single cumulative impact.

## Common Traps

- **Literal calque**: Transferring source syntax directly into the target language, creating awkward translationese.
- **Lost register**: Flattening raw, casual, or vulgar street lyrics into polite formal prose, or turning delicate poetic verse into dry reportage.
- **Orthographic confusion**: Misinterpreting diacritics, accents, or regional variants. Always verify context.
