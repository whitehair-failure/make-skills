# Italian Source Lyrics

## Read Before Translating

- **Vocalic Purity & Bel Canto Flow**: Almost all Italian words end in vowels, facilitating continuous vocal legato and seamless musical lineation.
- **Troncamento & Elisione**: Traditional and modern Italian lyrics frequently clip final vowel sounds before consonants (*amor* for *amore*, *cuor* for *cuore*, *andar* for *andare*, *man* for *mano*) to adapt words to the melodic beat.
- **Pro-Drop & Clitic Pronominal Bundles**: Subject pronouns are omitted unless emphatic (*io*, *tu*); object clitics bundle before finite verbs (*te lo dico*, *se ne va*) or attach as enclitics to infinitives/imperatives (*dimmelo*, *lasciami*).
- **Poetic Inversion (Iperbato)**: Italian lyrics (especially in opera, Sanremo pop, and singer-songwriter *cantautori*) freely alter word order for rhyme and emotional emphasis (*dolce mia sposa*, *il tempo che ci resta*).

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain:

- A clipped infinitive or noun whose full syntactic role resolves across lines.
- Prepositional chains (*nel mezzo del... / cammino*) split by melodic rests.
- Relative clauses introduced by *che*, *cui*, *il quale*.
- Complex conditional periods (*se potessi... / ti darei...*).

## Common Traps

- Do not mistake clipped poetic forms (*man*, *fianco*, *ciel*, *sol*) for errors or slang; they are classical lyric conventions.
- Beware of Italian–English / Italian–Spanish false friends (*attualmente* = currently; *salire* = to go up, not to leave; *burro* = butter, not donkey).
- Preserve the operatic/lyrical pathos without turning it into melodramatic parody.
