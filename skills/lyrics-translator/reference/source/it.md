# Italian Source Lyrics

## Read Before Translating

- **Vocalic Purity & Bel Canto Flow**: Almost all Italian words end in vowels, facilitating continuous vocal legato and seamless musical lineation.
- **Troncamento & Elisione**: Traditional and modern Italian lyrics frequently clip final vowel sounds before consonants (_amor_ for _amore_, _cuor_ for _cuore_, _andar_ for _andare_, _man_ for _mano_) to adapt words to the melodic beat.
- **Pro-Drop & Clitic Pronominal Bundles**: Subject pronouns are omitted unless emphatic (_io_, _tu_); object clitics bundle before finite verbs (_te lo dico_, _se ne va_) or attach as enclitics to infinitives/imperatives (_dimmelo_, _lasciami_).
- **Poetic Inversion (Iperbato)**: Italian lyrics (especially in opera, Sanremo pop, and singer-songwriter _cantautori_) freely alter word order for rhyme and emotional emphasis (_dolce mia sposa_, _il tempo che ci resta_).

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain:

- A clipped infinitive or noun whose full syntactic role resolves across lines.
- Prepositional chains (_nel mezzo del... / cammino_) split by melodic rests.
- Relative clauses introduced by _che_, _cui_, _il quale_.
- Complex conditional periods (_se potessi... / ti darei..._).

## Common Traps

- Do not mistake clipped poetic forms (_man_, _fianco_, _ciel_, _sol_) for errors or slang; they are classical lyric conventions.
- Beware of Italian–English / Italian–Spanish false friends (_attualmente_ = currently; _salire_ = to go up, not to leave; _burro_ = butter, not donkey).
- Preserve the operatic/lyrical pathos without turning it into melodramatic parody.
