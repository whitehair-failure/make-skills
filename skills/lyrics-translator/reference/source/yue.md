# Cantonese Source Lyrics (粤语歌词)

## Read Before Translating

- **Nine Tones & Six Categories (九声六调)**: Cantonese has a rich tonal system including checked tones with unreleased plosive stops (入声韵: -p, -t, -k). In Cantopop, lyrics must "harmonize with tones" (协音) — the musical pitch contour must match the linguistic tone contours, otherwise "tone inversion" (倒字) occurs.
- **Written Form Duality**:
  - *Standard Written Chinese register (书面语/文言笔意)*: Traditional Cantopop (James Wong 黄霑, Lo Kwok-chim 卢国沾, Richard Lam 林振强, Lin Xi 林夕, Wyman Wong 黄伟文) primarily writes in poetic standard Chinese but pronounced in Cantonese phonology.
  - *Vernacular Cantonese register (纯口语/白话入词)*: Featured in hip-hop, humorous songs, or modern indie tracks using dedicated Cantonese characters (嘅, 咗, 緊, 仲, 唔, 咁, 哋, 乜, 點).
- **Tone of Address & Emotional Restraint**: Cantopop often features poignant urban alienation, bittersweet nostalgia, and subtle emotional irony.

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain:

- Classical poetic parallelism (对仗) or split couplets spanning across two musical bars.
- Verb-aspect markers or directional complements carried into the following line.
- Dense metaphoric imagery where the vehicle and tenor span adjacent phrases.
- Refrains where rhyme scheme locks multiple lines together into an inseparable emotional unit.

## Common Traps

- Do not mistake standard Chinese characters in Cantopop for Mandarin grammar; the phrasing often carries subtle Cantonese rhythmic cadences and classical Hong Kong cultural allusions.
- When vernacular Cantonese appears (e.g. *原谅我这一生不羁放纵爱自由* vs. *做人冇梦想同条咸鱼有咩分别*), identify whether the lyric is lyrical-elevated or gritty-vernacular.
- Pay attention to checked-tone rhymes (入声: 伯/客/魄, 骨/不/雪) which deliver abrupt, percussive musical impacts.
