# Russian Source Lyrics

## Read Before Translating

- **Dynamic Stress & Vowel Reduction**: Russian has free, mobile stress (_ударение_). Unstressed vowels undergo severe qualitative reduction (e.g., unstressed _o_ sounds like _a_). In songwriting, stress dictates the exact metrical beat; misreading stress distorts both meaning and rhyme.
- **Verbal Aspect (Вид глагола)**: The choice between Imperfective (_НСВ_ - ongoing process, habit, repetition) and Perfective (_СВ_ - completion, result, decisive single act) is crucial for the song's dramatic temporality.
- **Case System & Free Word Order**: Russian uses six grammatical cases. Word order is driven by functional sentence perspective (theme/rheme and emotional focus), not rigid SVO rules.
- **Omission of the Copula**: Present-tense "to be" (_есть_) is almost always omitted (_Я здесь_ = "I [am] here").
- **Poetic Melancholy & Rock Archetypes**: Russian rock (e.g., Kino, DDT, Zemfira) and romances (_романсы_) are known for dense existential metaphor, philosophical fatalism, and raw emotional candor (_тоска_, _душа_, _судьба_).

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain:

- A participle (_причастие_) or verbal adverb (_деепричастие_) completed on the next line.
- Enjambed subordinate clauses governed by _что_, _чтобы_, _когда_, _если_, _хотя_.
- Case-governed prepositional phrases (_вместо того, чтобы..._) split across rhythmic bars.
- Staccato verbless lines that collectively build a single cinematic panorama.

## Common Traps

- Do not mistake Russian verbless or impersonal sentences (_мне холодно_, _нет сил_) for incomplete grammar; they are complete, powerful emotional statements.
- Avoid mechanical word-order translation; reconstruct the communicative core before rendering into target languages.
- Be careful with diminutives (_ноченька_, _сердечко_): they denote intimacy or sorrow, not literal physical tininess.
