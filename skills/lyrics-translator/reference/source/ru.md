# Russian Source Lyrics

## Read Before Translating

- **Dynamic Stress & Vowel Reduction**: Russian has free, mobile stress (*ударение*). Unstressed vowels undergo severe qualitative reduction (e.g., unstressed *o* sounds like *a*). In songwriting, stress dictates the exact metrical beat; misreading stress distorts both meaning and rhyme.
- **Verbal Aspect (Вид глагола)**: The choice between Imperfective (*НСВ* - ongoing process, habit, repetition) and Perfective (*СВ* - completion, result, decisive single act) is crucial for the song's dramatic temporality.
- **Case System & Free Word Order**: Russian uses six grammatical cases. Word order is driven by functional sentence perspective (theme/rheme and emotional focus), not rigid SVO rules.
- **Omission of the Copula**: Present-tense "to be" (*есть*) is almost always omitted (*Я здесь* = "I [am] here").
- **Poetic Melancholy & Rock Archetypes**: Russian rock (e.g., Kino, DDT, Zemfira) and romances (*романсы*) are known for dense existential metaphor, philosophical fatalism, and raw emotional candor (*тоска*, *душа*, *судьба*).

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain:

- A participle (*причастие*) or verbal adverb (*деепричастие*) completed on the next line.
- Enjambed subordinate clauses governed by *что*, *чтобы*, *когда*, *если*, *хотя*.
- Case-governed prepositional phrases (*вместо того, чтобы...*) split across rhythmic bars.
- Staccato verbless lines that collectively build a single cinematic panorama.

## Common Traps

- Do not mistake Russian verbless or impersonal sentences (*мне холодно*, *нет сил*) for incomplete grammar; they are complete, powerful emotional statements.
- Avoid mechanical word-order translation; reconstruct the communicative core before rendering into target languages.
- Be careful with diminutives (*ноченька*, *сердечко*): they denote intimacy or sorrow, not literal physical tininess.
