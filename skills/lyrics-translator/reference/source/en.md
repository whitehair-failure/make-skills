# English Source Lyrics

## Read Before Translating

- Resolve contractions, phrasal verbs, idioms, and colloquial ellipsis by meaning, never word by word.
- Track explicit pronouns, tense, negation, and modality. Do not introduce a stronger commitment, causality, or emotional claim than the English states.
- Preserve repeated hooks and parallel structures with matching brevity in the target language.

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain any of these:

- Enjambment: a modifier, preposition, conjunction, or object is completed on the next line.
- An unfinished phrase or a phrasal verb split across lines.
- An anaphoric sequence whose referent or conclusion appears later.
- A parallel list or repeated hook whose intended rhythm depends on the neighbouring lines.

## Common Traps

- Render idioms and phrasal verbs by intended meaning.
- Lyrics frequently truncate or twist idioms, slang, and catchphrases in hooks; recover the full expression and its era/register before translating, and never invent a matching second half.
- Treat `you`, `we`, and `I` as context-sensitive in targets that omit or differentiate pronouns.
- Do not expand contracted, casual wording into formal exposition.
