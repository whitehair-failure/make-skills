# Vietnamese Source Lyrics

## Read Before Translating

- **Monosyllabic Tonal Language**: Vietnamese is an isolating language where each word corresponds to a single morpheme/syllable with one of six tones (Ngang, Huyền, Sắc, Hỏi, Ngã, Nặng).
- **Extremely High Information Density**: Like Chinese, each Vietnamese syllable packs dense semantic information. A brief musical phrase can convey a complete poetic concept.
- **Intricate Kinship Pronouns**: Song lyrics heavily utilize kinship terms as pronouns (_anh_ older brother / boyfriend / husband; _em_ younger sibling / girlfriend / wife; _tôi_ neutral; _mình_ intimate self/us). Resolve who is singing to whom to understand the emotional dynamic.
- **Sino-Vietnamese Vocabulary (Từ Hán Việt)**: Distinguish between vernacular words (pure Vietnamese: earthy, tender, colloquial) and Sino-Vietnamese words (classical, philosophical, solemn, formal).
- **Tone–Melody Interaction**: Traditional and contemporary songs respect the contour of spoken tones (_Bằng/Trắc_) so lyrics do not sound distorted when sung.

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain:

- Parallel couplets where meaning or emotional imagery is distributed across two balanced lines.
- Reduplicated descriptive words (_từ láy_: _lung linh_, _thao thức_, _xao xuyến_) whose predicate resolves across lines.
- Conjunctions indicating contrast or condition (_dù_, _nếu_, _bởi vì_, _cho dù_) carried into the next line.
- Serial verbal expressions describing a single emotional action.

## Common Traps

- Do not translate _anh_ and _em_ mechanically as brother and sister in romantic ballads.
- Avoid treating monosyllabic lines as disconnected sentences; verify the overarching poetic image.
- Watch out for false cognates with Chinese or French loanwords.
