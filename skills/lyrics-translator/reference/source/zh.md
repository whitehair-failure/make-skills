# Chinese Source Lyrics

## Read Before Translating

- Resolve topic–comment structure, omitted subjects and objects, aspect markers (`了`, `着`, `过`), and modal adverbs from the surrounding block.
- Interpret 成语, classical allusions, and fixed imagery by meaning and function, not character by character.
- Preserve the rhetorical role of reduplication and parallelism; do not mistake compact poetic cuts for complete independent sentences.

## Semantic-Chunking Signals

Make one semantic block when consecutive lines contain any of these:

- A topic, modifier, or object whose predicate appears in the next line.
- Parallel short lines that collectively establish a single image or predicate.
- An omitted subject, tense, or reference that cannot be resolved on the current line alone.
- A classical or idiomatic phrase completed or contrasted by the next line.

## Common Traps

- Infer subjects only when the block makes them clear; otherwise preserve the ambiguity.
- Do not translate `了` mechanically as past tense or `已经`.
- Do not turn four-character compression into explanatory prose.
- Recognize lyrics that truncate or repurpose 成语 or classical allusions; resolve the full idiom's intent before translating, and watch connotation drift in internet-slang phrasing.
