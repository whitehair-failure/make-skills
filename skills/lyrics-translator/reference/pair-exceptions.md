# Pair Exceptions

Read only the entry matching the selected direction. These rules are exceptions; source and target files remain authoritative for general grammar and style.

## JA → ZH and ZH → JA

- Treat visually shared hanzi/kanji as potential false friends. Verify the Japanese or Chinese meaning before translating; never infer it from glyph shape.
- Preserve classical imagery by function and tone, not by forcing a character-for-character equivalent.

## JA → KO and KO → JA

- Map Japanese sentence-final particles and Korean endings by interpersonal tone, not fixed one-to-one suffix substitution.
- Translate onomatopoeia for effect; choose a conventional counterpart only when it fits the lyric’s register and rhythm.

## ZH → KO

- Translate Chinese 成语 and compact allusions by intended meaning. Do not use Sino-Korean-looking forms merely because the source uses hanzi.

## EN → ZH, EN → JA, EN → KO

- Resolve English articles, tense, pronouns, and phrasal verbs for meaning; do not preserve English word order.

## ZH → EN, JA → EN, KO → EN

- Supply English grammatical subjects and links only when required for a natural line. Preserve ambiguity when the source does not determine the referent.
